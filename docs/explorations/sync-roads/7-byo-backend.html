Road 7 of 10 · custody by absence

Bring your own backend

You ship the sync client. The user supplies the storage — their R2 bucket, their S3, their NAS. Canvases sync across their devices and to their agents, and you never hold a byte. Not because it's encrypted, but because it was never yours.

What you get

  • Custody eliminated by construction. Road 5's promise without Road 5's cryptography. There is no key to lose, no recovery phrase, no ciphertext to relay — the data simply lives somewhere you have no credentials for.
  • Cloud agents work. This is the one Road 5 couldn't do. An agent runs with the user's bucket credentials, on their infra or on a runner they authorise, reading plaintext they already own. You get remote agent edits without ever holding readable work.
  • Enterprise unblocks itself. "Where does our data live?" — in your bucket, in your region, under your retention policy. The security review you'd otherwise spend a year answering becomes a config screen.
  • Plaintext at rest is a feature here. Because it's their storage, their own tooling works on it: grep, backup, versioning, lifecycle rules, their own search index. Road 5 forecloses all of that; this road hands it back.
  • Ops cost near zero. No storage bill, no bandwidth bill, no retention policy, no deletion requests, no on-call for someone else's canvases.

The hard part: object storage is not a sync protocol

Buckets have no coordination primitive

There's no locking, no ordering, no notification. Naive last-writer-wins on whole .canvas files loses work the first time two devices are open at once.

The shape that works is an append-only log of Yjs updates, one object per client per batch — because CRDT updates merge in any order, so a bucket that only ever gains objects is a valid transport:

spaces/<space>/<canvas>/
  snapshot.bin          ← periodic compaction
  updates/<clientId>/0007.bin
  updates/<clientId>/0008.bin
  assets/<sha256>

Each client writes only under its own prefix, so writes never collide. Reading is a list + fetch of what you haven't seen. Compaction is any client rewriting snapshot.bin and deleting folded-in updates. Sync latency is your poll interval — minutes by default, seconds if someone's watching.

What you give up

  • The setup wall. The honest failure mode of this entire road. If the answer to "how do I sync?" is "create an R2 bucket and paste an access key," most designers stop there. Everything else on this page is downstream of solving that.
  • Live co-editing and presence. Polling a bucket gives you sync, not a session. Two people in one canvas at once is not this road.
  • N backends to support. Every provider has its own auth, its own CORS quirks, its own listing semantics. Ship one blessed provider and one generic S3-compatible field, or this becomes the whole job.
  • You can't help when it breaks. Their bucket, their credentials, their misconfigured lifecycle rule that deleted last month's updates. Support without access is a different discipline.
  • A share link for people who don't own the app — unless the bucket serves one. See Road 9; it's more tractable here than it looks.

Cheaper than Road 2 and far cheaper than Road 5, because the expensive half of E2EE — key management, device enrolment, recovery — evaporates. The cost moves to onboarding and to supporting infrastructure you can't see.

The hosted-default question

The same client, pointed at a bucket you operate, is Road 4's custody with none of Road 4's architecture. That's tempting and it's the obvious way over the setup wall: hosted by default, BYO for anyone who asks.

Be honest about what it costs. The moment the default user's canvases sit in your bucket, the claim degrades from "we cannot read your work" to "you can opt out of us." Those are different products with different trust stories, and the second one is the one you'd actually be shipping to most people.

Who does this

Obsidian's Remotely Save and Self-hosted LiveSync. Anytype. Syncthing. Joplin with a custom WebDAV target. Zotero with your own storage. Bitwarden self-hosted. In every case the sophisticated minority self-hosts and the majority takes the vendor's bucket — which tells you what the adoption curve looks like.

The strategic read: the only road that delivers cloud agents and zero custody at the same time, which is precisely the combination the other six couldn't produce. It buys that by converting a product decision into a customer setup task — and the conversion rate on setup tasks is brutal. Worth building the driver; be very careful about making it the only door in.
