Road 1 of 6 · no cloud

Solo vault

Specular never talks to a server. A space is a folder on your disk. Sharing means handing someone files. Multiplayer does not exist, and that is the point.

Shape

Click a box.

What you get

  • Absolute privacy. Not a policy or an encryption scheme — an absence. There is nothing to breach, subpoena, or leak.
  • No consent surface. No moment where a button quietly uploads someone's work.
  • Zero infrastructure. No Cloudflare bill, no on-call, no auth layer, no key management.
  • Agents still work. Claude Code on your machine already drives the canvas over the local CLI. The single most valuable form of "multiplayer" needs no cloud at all.
  • Nothing rots. The app works identically in ten years with no company behind it.

What you give up

  • Device sync, unless you delegate it to iCloud/Dropbox/git — which is a real trap: those tools sync files on a timer while the app is writing them, and a half-written .canvas is a corrupted canvas.
  • Any share link. Showing your work means exporting or screen-sharing.
  • Remote agents. An agent on someone else's machine can't reach your canvas.
  • A recurring revenue model. This is a one-time-purchase app.

Who does this

Obsidian without Sync. Sublime Text. Affinity. Most software before 2015 — and a growing amount of software after 2024, as people notice the subscription treadmill.

The honest question: is the thing you're building a tool one person uses to think, or a place several people meet? If it's genuinely the former, every other road on this list is optional — and the cost of skipping them is one feature request you decline, repeatedly, forever.
