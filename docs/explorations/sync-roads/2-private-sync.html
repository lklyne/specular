Private sync

An account syncs your spaces across your own devices. The server stores ciphertext it cannot read. No sharing, no multiplayer, no web view — the sync is a backup and a commute, not a meeting place.

What you get

  • A privacy claim you can put on the pricing page and mean literally: we cannot read your work.
  • A subscription business that doesn't require you to become a social product.
  • Version history and real backup — arguably more valuable day to day than sharing.
  • Multi-device, which for a desktop-heavy tool is the most-requested feature that isn't collaboration.

What you give up

  • Everything social. No links, no comments from people who don't own the app, no web viewing.
  • Cloud agents. An agent that isn't on your machine can't decrypt anything.
  • Server-side anything — no thumbnails, no search, no rendering, no "open in browser."
The support cost people underestimate: end-to-end encryption means losing the key means losing the data, permanently, with you unable to help. Every E2EE product either builds a recovery-phrase flow users hate, or eats angry support tickets forever. Budget for it as a product surface, not a footnote.

Who does this

Obsidian Sync. Apple Notes over iCloud. Standard Notes. 1Password. All of them sell trust as the feature, and all of them took years to add any sharing at all.

The strategic read: this is the cheapest road to a real business that doesn't compromise the privacy story. It's also a one-way door in the opposite direction from Road 4 — once you've told people you can't read their work, adding a server that renders it is a promise you have to break.
