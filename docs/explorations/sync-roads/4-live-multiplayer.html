Live multiplayer

One shared document per canvas, living in the cloud. Humans, browser viewers and agents all connect as equal peers and edit at the same time. The server reads everything, and that is what makes the rest possible.

Click a box.

What you get

  • Everything. Live co-editing, cursors, a browser client that can actually render, cloud agents, server-side screenshots, thumbnails, search, recovery.
  • One substrate, many features. Device sync and multiplayer are literally the same code path with a different number of participants.
  • The familiar product. Nobody needs the model explained; it's how Figma works.
  • Mostly built. The spike already has the sync server, capability links, asset storage, and a headless agent peer working.

What you give up

  • Custody-free positioning. "Local-first" becomes "local-capable." You hold readable copies of your users' work, forever, at rest.
  • A quiet life. Breach exposure, retention policy, employee access controls, deletion requests, DPAs the moment one company adopts it.
  • Reversibility. A link, once opened, gave someone a complete replica. Revocation stops future sync; it does not recall data.
Two sharp edges specific to this product, not to multiplayer in general:
  • A shared canvas full of live pages pointed at localhost renders against the other person's localhost — the same URL, a different site, no warning.
  • A peer with an edit link can write an HTML entity into your canvas, which your app then renders. That is someone else's arbitrary code executing inside your desktop app.

The sync was the easy part and it's done. The auth layer, the web client, attribution, multiplayer undo, and the entire duty-of-care surface are ahead.

Who does this

Figma, Miro, Notion, Linear. Every one of them is a custodian, and every one of them has an enterprise trust page, a security team, and a breach plan.

The strategic read: the most capable road and the only genuinely irreversible one. Roads 1, 2, 3 and 5 can each become another later. This one, once real users' work is sitting readable on your server, is where you stay.
